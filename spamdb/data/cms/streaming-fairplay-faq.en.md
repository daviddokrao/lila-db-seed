# Fair Play While Streaming

Streaming your games is welcome. It does create two problems that do not exist
when you play privately.

## Your viewers can see your board

Anyone watching your stream can run an engine on the position and tell you the
move in chat. **You do not have to ask for it — receiving it is still assistance**,
and the game record looks the same either way.

If a viewer posts a move or an evaluation, do not use it, and moderate it out.
Repeatedly playing on with a chat that is feeding you moves is treated as engine
use, because in effect it is.

## Your opponent can watch you

Someone playing you can open your stream and see your board, your clock and your
face. With a delay of a few seconds this is a real advantage, especially in
bullet.

Two ways to handle it:

- **Add a stream delay** of at least the length of your games. This is the usual
  choice and it costs you nothing
- **Hide the board while playing** and review afterwards

Neither is required. But if you stream live with no delay, expect it to happen,
and do not report the opponent for watching a public broadcast.

## Practical rules

- **Do not analyse your own live game on stream**, even out loud to yourself with
  no engine. Chat will finish the thought for you
- **Turn off engine overlays** while playing. Leave them for review afterwards
- **Do not let a guest "help"** during a game

## Titled players and events

Some tournaments require a delay, or forbid streaming entirely while playing.
That is set by the organiser; read the tournament description.
