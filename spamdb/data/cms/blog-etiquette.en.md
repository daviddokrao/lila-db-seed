# Blog Etiquette

Your blog is yours. These are the limits.

## What belongs here

Chess. Your games, openings you are learning, tournaments you played, books you
read, positions that puzzled you. Writing about the site itself is fine too.

## What does not

- **Anything covered by the [Communication Guidelines](/page/communication-guidelines)** —
  hate, harassment, threats, sexual content. A blog is not a private space
- **Advertising**, affiliate links, or posts that exist to drive traffic somewhere
  else
- **Work that is not yours.** Quoting a book or another author's analysis is fine
  with attribution; reprinting a chapter is not
- **Accusations against other players.** Naming someone as a cheat in a post is
  not evidence and is not the process. Use the [report form](/report)
- **Engine output presented as analysis.** Pasting a variation the engine gave you
  is not a post; explaining why the engine is right might be

## If your blog is hidden

Posts can be hidden from public listings while staying visible to you. That
usually means the content broke one of the rules above, or the blog was being
used to drive traffic rather than to write.

You can appeal — see [Appealing a Decision](/page/appeal). Rewriting the post to
fix the problem is usually faster than arguing about it.

## Comments on your posts

Enabling discussion creates a forum thread attached to the post. You are
responsible for what stays up there, the same way team leaders are for their team
forum. Moderate it or turn discussion off.

Practical advice on writing is in [Blog Tips](/page/blog-tips).
